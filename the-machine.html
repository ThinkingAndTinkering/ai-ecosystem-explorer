01 · Why this page exists

The discourse skips straight to "AGI" or "bubble," as if the machine in between were a detail.

It isn't. Between your chat window and the answer sits the most expensive industrial project in history: one Dutch company's light machines, one Taiwanese island's fabs, three memory makers, one chip designer with a 75% gross margin, an optics supply chain that is literally sold out, gas turbines queued to 2030, restarted nuclear plants, and four companies spending a Belgium-sized GDP a year on all of it.

Every confident take — "it's the new electricity", "it's Pets.com with cooling towers" — is built on one cherry-picked fact. The honest position requires holding the whole machine in your head at once. That's hard. So this page does three things:

1.  Puts the money side-by-side (§02) — what goes in vs. what comes out.
2.  Hands you the two load-bearing models — power (§03) and payback (§04) — as live calculators.
3.  Walks the entire supply chain, all 61 public-and-private pieces of it, layer by layer (§06).

It's not obviously stupid, and it's not a sure thing. Anyone who tells you otherwise hasn't done the arithmetic — so let's do the arithmetic.

spend-to-revenue · the number this whole page interrogates

Two readings of the same ratio. Bear: no industry has ever sustained 10:1 — this is tulips with liquid cooling. Bull: every network in history (rail, electricity, telecom, cloud) ran spend-first, and unlike dot-com telecoms, the spenders fund this from ~$100B+ a quarter of operating cash flow while demand is observably supply-constrained — Azure +40%, Google Cloud +63%, AWS +28%, with $1.4T+ of contracted backlog among them.3

Who's right depends on two physical questions: can they power it (§03), and can revenue outrun depreciation (§04)?

04 · Calculator II

The Revenue Gap — does the math close?

Chips depreciate. Whatever you believe about superintelligence, the accountants will book roughly capex ÷ useful-life as a cost every year, forever. Revenue has to outrun that treadmill. Michael Burry's short thesis says useful life is really 2–3 years and earnings are overstated by ~$176B; the hyperscalers say ~5–6 years and point to 2020-era GPUs still earning their keep.8 Pick your own numbers:

Model & assumptions (click to inspect)
Year 0 = 2026. Cost line: cost(t) = min(t,life) × spend ÷ life × 1.2 — the fleet builds until it's life years deep, then depreciation plateaus; ×1.2 adds operating costs. Revenue line: rev(t) = rev₀ × (1+g)ᵗ, with growth fading 15% per year (nothing compounds at 60% forever). "Closes" = revenue ≥ cost. Deliberately ignores: margins between layers, financing costs, and the possibility that cheaper intelligence creates demand we can't imagine (the Jevons case). It's an envelope, not a DCF — break it.

Honest takeaways from playing with this: at hyperscaler depreciation schedules (5–6 yrs) and current growth, the math closes around the turn of the decade. At Burry's 2–3 years, it needs growth nobody's modeling, indefinitely. The useful-life debate is the bubble debate.

06 · The supply chain, company by company

61 pieces of the machine

Market caps are June 11–12, 2026 (private valuations as dated); each card's stat is from the latest reported quarter. Click any card for its strength / risk. The 12-mo badge is the stock's one-year move where verified — it doubles as a map of where the market thinks the bottlenecks are.

1 · Design & Equipment

7 companies · the tools behind the tools

Everything begins with one company: ASML is the only maker of the EUV lithography machines that print advanced chips — export-banned to China entirely. Around it: the deposition/etch/inspection trio (Applied Materials, Lam, KLA — Lam up >4× in a year on the memory boom), the EDA software duopoly (Synopsys, Cadence) every chip is designed in, and Arm, whose architecture is in ~99% of phones and, increasingly, every hyperscaler CPU.

2 · Fabrication

3 companies · where atoms get printed

TSMC prints ~70% of the world's foundry output and ~90% of the leading edge at a 50.5% net margin — the single most load-bearing company in the machine, on the single most contested island. Samsung fights on every front at once (foundry #2, memory, devices). SMIC is China's sanctioned national champion, pushing 7nm without EUV at painful yields.

3 · Memory & Storage

4 companies · the supercycle

AI chips starve without data, and 2026's defining shortage lives here. HBM is a three-firm oligopoly (SK Hynix ~⅔ of NVIDIA's HBM4, Micron, plus Samsung) — all sold out, with DRAM prices up ~95% in a single quarter. One level down, AI data lakes broke the hard-drive market: Western Digital (+924% in 12 months) is sold out for 2026, Seagate (+618%) allocated into 2027.10 Your next laptop costs more because of this row.

4 · Chips & Accelerators

7 companies · the profit pool

NVIDIA — $81.6B in a quarter, ~75% gross margin, ~zero China revenue — remains the sun the system orbits. The resistance: Broadcom builds the hyperscalers' custom chips ($10.8B AI revenue last quarter, +143%) and triggered June 5's trillion-dollar selloff with one soft guide; AMD is the merchant challenger with OpenAI's 6GW deal; Intel is the state-backed comeback (+~550% in a year); Monolithic Power quietly powers every kilowatt-class GPU socket.

5 · Networking & Optics

7 companies · the nervous system

A 100,000-GPU cluster is only as smart as its wiring, and this layer produced 2026's wildest charts. Arista switches the hyperscalers' AI fabrics (AI target: $3.5B). Astera Labs rides inside NVIDIA racks; Credo's cables grew revenue 206% in a year. The optics squeeze is historic: Lumentum's lasers are sold out (+959% in 12 months), Coherent's datacom grew 40%, Ciena (+524%) carries AI traffic between campuses, and Amphenol connects all of it ($9.4B of orders in one quarter).

6 · Systems & Data Centers

7 companies · racks, shells, cooling

Someone has to bolt the machine together. Dell booked $24.4B of AI-server orders in a single quarter (~$51B backlog); Super Micro is the fast-turn specialist; Celestica builds the hyperscalers' switches. Inside the building: Vertiv (power & cooling, backlog +109%, co-designing 800-volt racks with NVIDIA) and Eaton (electrical gear, data-center orders up ~240%). The landlords: Equinix and Digital Realty, whose record leasing now waits on grid connections, not customers.

7 · Power & Energy

6 companies · the binding constraint

The layer Wall Street discovered last. GE Vernova's turbine queue hit ~100 GW — slots essentially gone until 2030. The nuclear fleet signed directly with Big Tech: Constellation restarts Three Mile Island for Microsoft (2027) and powers Meta from Clinton; Vistra sells Meta 2.6 GW; Talen sends Amazon 1.92 GW through 2042. NextEra builds renewables at 33-GW-backlog scale, and Oklo — pre-revenue, ~14 GW of letters of intent — is the option on small reactors. Electricity bills already swing elections; this layer is where AI meets politics.11

8 · Clouds

7 companies · the $725B/yr spenders

The hyperscalers turn capex into rentable intelligence: Microsoft (Azure +40%, $627B backlog), Amazon (AWS +28%, >$20B/yr of in-house chips), Alphabet (Cloud +63%, the only full-stack player), and Oracle — the leveraged bet, with a $638B backlog mostly from one customer, negative free cash flow, and a stock 47% off its high. Below them, the neoclouds: CoreWeave ($99B backlog), Nebius (revenue +684%), and IREN (Microsoft's $9.7B GB300 deal) — the high-beta tissue of the boom.

9 · Models & Intelligence

7 labs · where capex becomes capability

The labs convert all of the above into tokens. OpenAI: ~900M weekly users, $852B valuation, IPO filed confidentially. Anthropic: $30B+ run-rate, a $965B Series H — the most valuable startup on Earth — and the leaderboard crown as of last Tuesday. Google DeepMind needs no one's chips or cloud. xAI lives inside SpaceX; Meta's lab went closed-source; Mistral carries Europe; DeepSeek proves what China can do with constrained silicon and open weights.

10 · Applications & Devices

6 companies · where it touches you

The top of the machine is strangely the cheapest seat: Apple rents its assistant's brain from Google for ~$1B/yr while spending 7% of a hyperscaler's capex — and trades near all-time highs. Meta monetizes AI through ads at $56B a quarter. Tesla bets on intelligence-in-motion. Palantir grew 85% and still fell — perfection was pre-priced. And the SaaS incumbents (Salesforce, ServiceNow) wear 2026's deepest scar: ~$2T of enterprise-software value erased by the agent question.12

Model & assumptions
TWh = Wh × queries/day × users × 365 ÷ 10¹². Treats weekly users as daily-equivalent users (generous). Excludes training, fine-tuning, video, and idle capacity — the loads that actually dominate. Per-query energy keeps falling (−33×/yr at Google) while volume keeps rising faster: the Jevons treadmill in one slider.

Choke-point inventory (the short version)

EUV: one supplier (ASML). Leading-edge fabrication: one island (~90%, TSMC). HBM: three firms, sold out. Advanced packaging: one bottleneck (CoWoS, doubling again). Datacom lasers: sold out (Lumentum, Coherent). Nearline drives: allocated to 2027. H-class turbines: queued to ~2030. Grid interconnects: years. Each is a single point of failure nobody designed — efficiency built this machine; resilience was nobody's job. A longer treatment, with the geopolitics, is in the companion handbook's Chapter 5.

The watchlist — five signals that decide who's right

This page is a snapshot of June 2026. These are the falsifiable indicators that will confirm or break its models — check them against any future quarter:

signal 01 · backlog → cash
Do the $1.4T of cloud backlogs convert to revenue on schedule — especially Oracle's $638B from one customer? On-time conversion validates the buildout; slippage is the first crack.
signal 02 · lab gross margins
If OpenAI/Anthropic margins climb toward software economics as they scale, the Revenue Gap (§04) closes early. Flat margins mean the depreciation treadmill is winning.
signal 03 · power milestones
Crane/Three Mile Island restarting on time in 2027, turbine deliveries holding schedule, grid interconnects clearing. Slips here push every other layer's plans right.
signal 04 · the China door
A working H200/B30A export regime would reopen tens of billions of annual demand; continued two-government stalemate keeps NVIDIA's China revenue at ~$0 and accelerates the domestic counter-stack.
signal 05 · the growth slider
If agentic workloads hold real AI revenue growth above 60%/yr through 2028, §04's math closes under almost any depreciation assumption. Below ~30%, it closes under almost none.
